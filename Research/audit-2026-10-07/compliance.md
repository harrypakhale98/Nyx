## App Store compliance and privacy — findings

Reviewed 2026-10-07. Static review plus web research; nothing built or booted. Not legal advice; items marked "counsel" need a real lawyer or the rights holder.
Sources read today (all fetched 2026-10-07): Apple App Review Guidelines (page says "Last Updated: June 8, 2026", https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), Open-Meteo terms/licence/pricing (https://open-meteo.com/en/terms, /en/licence, /en/pricing), NPS API guides (https://www.nps.gov/subjects/developer/guides.htm) and NPS disclaimer (https://www.nps.gov/aboutus/disclaimer.htm), Apple App Privacy Details (https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/), Apple DSA trader page, Apple Texas news (https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=sg176nne, https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=8jzbigf4) and age-assurance Q&A (https://developer.apple.com/support/age-assurance).

### Verdict
The privacy engineering is unusually clean and I could not break it. Exactly three hosts are reachable by code (`SafeHTTP`, redirects refused, per-host toggle checked before each request), the Watch and Vision targets contain no network code, no CLGeocoder/MapKit/WeatherKit/CloudKit/PCC is used, all outbound links are plain `Link`s to Safari, and the required-reason API scan is clean (UserDefaults CA92.1/1C8F.1 is the only category used). "Data Not Collected" is defensible under Apple's own definition of "collect" (checked against the live page today). The gaps are in the paperwork around the data: Open-Meteo's CC BY attribution is not done the way Open-Meteo's licence page asks (a link next to every place its data is displayed, absent on Watch and in all widgets), there is no in-app privacy-policy link or contact, and the single embedded NPS key breaks NPS's own "don't share your key" guidance and will not survive launch-day traffic. One item is not in any repo document at all: Texas's app store age law has been in force since 2026-06-04 per Apple, and Apple tells every developer "you must check the age of the people using your app" in those regions. None of this is likely to cause a rejection by itself; most of it is cheap to fix before submission.

### Findings

- **[C1] Open-Meteo CC BY 4.0 attribution is incomplete, and the "non-commercial" basis has no written confirmation** — Severity: P0 (licence condition; Guideline 5.2.2 says authorization "must be provided upon request")
  - Evidence: https://open-meteo.com/en/licence says "You must include a link next to any location Open-Meteo data are displayed, for example `<a href="https://open-meteo.com/">Weather data by Open-Meteo.com</a>`" and "indicate if changes were made". In the app the only place beside forecast data is a plain, non-tappable caption `Text("Open-Meteo · updated …")` (`Nyx/Views/ParkViews.swift:347`). About the data has the sentence "Weather data: Open-Meteo, CC BY 4.0. License: creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/." as unlinked text (`Nyx/Views/SettingsAndLearn.swift:133`); the smoke paragraph (:136) names "CAMS via Open-Meteo" with no link either. No Open-Meteo credit exists anywhere in `NyxWatch*`, `NyxWidgets` (cloud-derived scores shown in widgets, Live Activity, share card, notifications) — grep for "Open-Meteo" in those targets returns nothing. `BrowserLink` (`Nyx/Views/SkyGlowViews.swift:170`) lists only globeatnight.org, so even adding a link means updating that list and `Scripts/verify_release.py`.
  - Non-commercial status: terms (https://open-meteo.com/en/terms) define non-commercial as "private or non-profit websites or apps that do not have subscriptions or advertising"; commercial = "apps that have subscriptions or display advertisements" and "commercial products or promotional activities". A free, ad-free, IAP-free app by an individual fits the first sentence. The risk is the third clause ("promotional activities"): Nyx runs a press kit, social posts, award entries and App Store featuring nominations. The terms are silent on app stores; the pricing FAQ says only "non-commercial use, rate-limited to 10,000 calls/day, no uptime guarantee". They also say "We reserve the right to block applications and IP addresses that misuse our service without prior notice." Nothing is in writing.
  - Why it matters: attribution is a hard licence condition (CC BY); losing the free tier ends cloud scoring (the 25% term and the whole forecast context). 5.2.2 lets App Review ask for proof of permission.
  - Recommendation: (1) Make "Weather data by Open-Meteo.com" a real `Link` (Safari) next to the cloud meter on park detail and on the About the data forecast and smoke blocks; add `open-meteo.com` to `BrowserLink` and the verify script. State the change explicitly: "averaged over the dark window". (2) Add a Credits screen on Watch (and a line on Vision if it ever shows forecast-derived data) naming Open-Meteo; widgets cannot hold a link, so credit in the app's About plus the widget gallery description is the reasonable manner. (3) Email info@open-meteo.com describing the app (free, no ads, no subscriptions, no IAP, ~6-hourly 63-park batches) and keep the reply as the 5.2.2 authorization. (4) Keep "no ads, no subscriptions, no IAP" a standing constraint (SUBMISSION.md already notes it).
  - Effort: S

- **[C2] Texas SB 2420 (and Utah, Louisiana) age-assurance duties are unaddressed anywhere in the repo** — Severity: P0 (legal decision needed before submission; low enforcement likelihood for this app, but not zero)
  - Evidence: grep for `DeclaredAgeRange`, `SB 2420`, `age assurance` over the repo outside Research returns only unrelated park text. Apple, 2026-06-03: "due to Texas law SB 2420 … developers must implement age assurance for new Apple Accounts in Texas" via Declared Age Range, Significant Change API (PermissionKit), StoreKit age-rating property, and App Store server notifications; effective 2026-06-04 after the Fifth Circuit stayed the injunction (https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=sg176nne; stay reported by MoFo and Pearl Cohen; the earlier 2025-12-23 pause is at https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=8jzbigf4). Apple's Q&A (https://developer.apple.com/support/age-assurance): "In those regions, you must check the age of the people using your app", no exemption by rating, and "consult your legal counsel" on significant changes. Law-firm summaries (Wiley, MoFo) list developer duties (assign ratings, tell the store of significant changes, use store age data, delete it after use), penalties via the AG and Texas DTPA; the only exemption mentioned is emergency-type apps. Utah's duties were delayed to 2027-05-06 (HB 498, 2026-03-18) and Louisiana's to 2027-07-01 (HB 977, 2026-05-15); both from secondary sources, unverified against statutes. Texas's status after June is unclear (Supreme Court petition mentioned by WLF 2026-06-18, unverified outcome).
  - SDK check: `DeclaredAgeRange.framework` is in the iPhoneOS 27.0 SDK, `AgeRangeService` iOS 26.0+, `visionOS` unavailable, with `isEligibleForAgeFeatures`, `requiredRegulatoryFeatures` and `requestAgeRange(ageGates:in:)` (`DeclaredAgeRange.swiftinterface` lines 12-14, 100-106).
  - Why it matters: civil exposure (private action under DTPA) for a free app with no age-restricted content is small, but Apple's own wording is categorical, and "Nyx collects nothing" does not answer a duty to "check age". No server means Nyx cannot receive App Store Server Notifications about parental revocation; counsel should say whether that matters for an all-ages utility.
  - Recommendation: one hour with counsel. If they say comply: on iOS 26+, read `requiredRegulatoryFeatures` / `isEligibleForAgeFeatures` at launch, request an age range only when the system says it is required, store nothing (Texas requires deletion), gate nothing (no restricted content), and document it in PRIVACY.md. Keep the age rating 4+ and re-answer the new questionnaire. Re-check Utah/Louisiana before 2027-05-06.
  - Effort: S to M

- **[C3] The shared NPS key: terms conflict, extractable, and a launch-day availability cliff** — Severity: P1
  - Evidence: NPS guide: "Keep your NPS API key private … don't share your API key or accidentally publish it" and "Hourly Limit: 1,000 requests per hour … applied across all developer.nps.gov API requests" for the key (https://www.nps.gov/subjects/developer/guides.htm). Nyx ships one key in cleartext in the app's Info.plist (`Config/Nyx-Info.plist`, `project.yml:42`), read by `PlanModel.npsKey` (`Nyx/App/PlanModel.swift:174`), and sends it as `api_key=` in the URL query (`Nyx/Services/DataServices.swift:325`) although the guide shows the `X-Api-Key` header form. Every install then spends the same 1,000/hour: `refreshParkUpdates` requests one park at a time (`PlanModel.swift:201-206`) for every park in the Tonight radius, and again on park detail and calendar. Backoff after 429 is an hour (`Backoff`), and the fallback is "Access not checked. Confirm closures with the park." (`PlanModel.swift:217`). INPUT_NEEDED #10 already asks NPS for a higher limit. I scanned `git log -p` for key patterns: none leaked; the key lives only in the git-ignored `Config/Secrets.xcconfig` and the IPA.
  - Why it matters: closures beside the score are the headline safety feature (the product brief §4). With a few dozen concurrent installs on a wide radius, the key is throttled for everyone, and closure data silently goes stale on exactly the busy nights. A published key can also be abused by third parties, getting it revoked.
  - Recommendation: (1) Fetch alerts for all 63 parks in one request: the NPS docs' own example is `alerts?parkCode=acad,dena`, so `parkCode` takes a comma list (paginate at `limit`). One or two requests per refresh instead of up to 63, and the request no longer depends on where the person is (see C9). (2) Send the key as `X-Api-Key`, not in the URL. (3) Ask NPS for a raised limit now and say the key ships in a public app (INPUT_NEEDED #10; make it a pre-submission step, not optional). (4) Optional: a "Use my own NPS key" field in Your privacy for heavy users.
  - Effort: S to M

  - Why it matters: NASA/SVS credit and Open-Meteo CC BY are conditions of use, and each app is reviewed and distributed as its own binary (the Vision app has a separate build).
  - Recommendation: add a one-screen "Credits" view to Watch and Vision (and a window button) with the same sentences as About the data, shortened. Add the LROC/LOLA line to every NASA Moon credit.
  - Effort: S

- **[C5] No in-app privacy-policy link, support or contact, or version/credits row** — Severity: P1 (Guidelines 5.1.1(i) "link to their privacy policy … within the app in an easily accessible manner", 1.5 "your app and its Support URL include an easy way to contact you")

- **[C6] Storefront and EU Digital Services Act trader decision is not recorded** — Severity: P2
  - Evidence: no mention of trader status or territory selection in SUBMISSION.md or INPUT_NEEDED.md (grep "trader|DSA|storefront" found only Mac availability and event territories). Apple's page: every developer must declare trader status; traders' address, phone and email are published on the EU product page; non-traders show no contact info and consumers are told consumer-law rights "won't apply" (https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-compliance-information/manage-european-union-digital-services-act-trader-requirements). The criteria include "develop the app in connection with your trade, business, craft, or profession": a portfolio app with press kit and award entries could reasonably be argued either way (counsel/owner).
  - Recommendation: launch in the US (plus Canada and Mexico, since Spanish (Mexico) is shipped) only; leave EU/UK out until the owner decides trader status and is willing to publish a P.O. box and phone number; untick China mainland (ICP filing; from memory, unverified today). The data is US-park-only, so the product loses little.
  - Effort: S

- **[C8] Store copy that states more than the data flow supports** — Severity: P2 (Guideline 2.3, accurate metadata/privacy)
  - Evidence: the App Store description says "Your location and your journal stay on your device … They receive the public coordinates of national parks, not yours" (`Store/1.1/metadata.md` PRIVATE BY DESIGN). The NPS host receives park codes only for parks the person opens or saves and parks inside the Tonight radius (`PlanModel.swift:201-206`, `Nyx/Views/SettingsAndLearn.swift:107`), which the policy itself says "can suggest a broad region" (`docs/privacy.html`). The Open-Meteo statement is exact (always all 63 parks: `PlanModel.swift:182-195`); the NPS one is not. The label reasoning in PRIVACY.md is right under Apple's definition, verified today: "collect" = transmitting data "in a way that allows you and/or your third-party partners to access it"; "third-party partners" = vendors "whose code you've added to your app" (https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/). I could not verify whether the api.data.gov key-owner portal shows the key owner per-request IP or park-code logs; confirm it does not, because "the developer receives nothing" depends on it.
  - Recommendation: fix at the source with C3 (one nationwide alerts request makes the sentence literally true). Until then say "park alerts are requested for the parks you open, save or have in range".
  - Effort: S

- **[C9] Open-Meteo footprint will meet its limits on shared IPs** — Severity: P2
  - Evidence: free limits 600/min, 5,000/hour, 10,000/day, "calls" weighted by locations/variables/days (https://open-meteo.com/en/pricing, "How is one API call defined?"). One Nyx refresh sends the 63-park forecast (17 days), a three-model request, a layers request (~7 variables) and an air-quality request, each in chunks of 50 (`DataServices.swift`, `ForecastDetailService.fetch`). My estimate is several hundred weighted calls per full refresh; the multi-location weighting is not spelled out, so that is unverified. The limit is not stated to be per IP in what I read (unverified), but campuses, hotels, tethered carriers and CGNAT will share addresses. A 429 triggers a one-hour backoff (`Backoff`).
  - Recommendation: fetch the model and layer detail only for saved and recently opened parks (it is a seven-day, context-only layer), keep the 63-park clouds request as is, add a "Forecast busy; showing the last one from …" calm state, and record the Open-Meteo reply from C1.
  - Effort: M

### Strengths worth protecting
- The `SafeHTTP` + `HostGuard` + toggle design and a release script that asserts it. This is the evidence Apple review and a journalist would ask for.
- A privacy policy that tells the truth about IP addresses and the radius-based NPS requests rather than hiding behind "Data Not Collected".

- Honest "Moon and darkness only" labelling, which keeps the metadata accurate by construction.
