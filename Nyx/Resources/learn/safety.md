Safe in the dark

The darkest skies are far from towns, and that distance is part of the plan. A few habits make a night in a park calm instead of risky.

Drive the last miles in daylight if you can. After dusk, deer, elk, cattle and smaller animals use park roads, and many are hard to see until they are close. Slow down, watch the shoulders for eyeshine, and use high beams only when no one is ahead of you. Stop only in pullouts and parking areas, never on the road itself, and turn your headlights off once you have parked.

Expect the cold. Clear, dry air lets the ground lose its heat quickly after sunset. In the desert and the mountains the temperature can fall 30°F (about 17°C) or more by midnight, even after a hot day, and sitting still makes it feel colder. Bring warm layers, a hat, gloves and something to sit on, and more water than you think you need.

Plan for no signal. Many parks have no cell coverage beyond their visitor centers. Save offline maps and the park's directions before you leave, and fill the tank. Nyx works offline once its data is on your phone, but it cannot call for help.

Tell someone your plan. Before you go, share where you will park, where you will walk and when you expect to be back. Agree on what they should do if they do not hear from you, and then keep to the plan.

Use a red light, and use it kindly. A dim red headlamp lets you see the ground without undoing your night vision or anyone else's. Keep it pointed down, never at faces or telescopes, and ask before switching on a white light near others. When safety calls for more light, on a steep trail or near a drop, use it. Your footing matters more than anyone's dark adaptation.

Read the park's alerts. Roads, trails and campgrounds close for floods, fire, snow, construction and wildlife. Nyx shows closures from the National Park Service beside every score, but they can be hours old, and some hazards never become alerts. Check the park's website before you leave, and ask a ranger where you may be after dark.

Leave no trace after dark. Stay on roads, trails and other durable surfaces; desert soil crusts and alpine plants can take years to recover from a single footprint. Keep food sealed and stored in your vehicle. Pack out every wrapper and battery. Let animals pass without holding them in a beam of light.

None of this needs to feel grim. Once you are prepared, you can stop thinking about the car, the cold and the way back, and give the sky your full attention.
