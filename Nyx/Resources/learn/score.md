Reading the Darkness Score

The Darkness Score answers one question: how dark and clear will the sky be at this park on this night? It runs from 0 to 100. Nyx builds it from four parts, then checks it against the weakest link.

The four parts have fixed weights. The Moon counts for 40 points: a night with no moonlight during true darkness earns all 40, and a full Moon high in the sky all night earns none. Clouds count for 25, averaged over the hours of true darkness. Sky glow counts for 20, from the park's estimated Bortle class. The length of true darkness counts for 15, and ten hours or more earns all of it. True darkness is the time when the Sun is more than 18 degrees below the horizon and twilight has left the sky.

Adding alone would mislead: a moonless night under thick cloud would still add up to a respectable number. So the score never rises above its weakest link. Cloud caps it: half cover holds a night to 55, and full overcast to 10. Sky glow caps it: a Bortle 3 park can reach 89 at most, so only the darkest skies earn Pristine. Smoke or haze in the forecast caps it at 74, or at 59 when it is heavy. A night with less than three hours of true darkness is capped too, and one with none at all stays below 40.

Here is one night. A Bortle 2 park, a thin crescent Moon that sets at dusk, nine hours of true darkness and a forecast of 40% cloud. The parts add up to 86: 40 for the Moon, 15 for clouds, 17.5 for sky glow and 13.5 for length. But 40% cloud caps the night at 64, so the score is 64, Good, and the breakdown says that clouds limit the night to 64.

Clouds are the least certain part. Up to about three days ahead, Nyx uses the cloud forecast in full. From there to ten days, it eases the forecast toward the park's usual clouds for that month and calls the night an early look, drawn half-filled in the calendar. Beyond ten days a forecast adds little, so the score rests on the usual clouds alone and the night is drawn hollow. Those scores will change as the night comes closer.

The bands are simple: 90 and above is Pristine, 75 Excellent, 60 Good, 40 Fair, and anything lower is Poor.

Some things the score cannot know. It does not know whether the road to your viewing spot is open; Nyx shows the park's alerts beside the score, but they can be hours old. It cannot see a lit parking lot, a lantern in a campground or a ridge that hides the southern sky. And it cannot know your eyes: how long they have had to adapt, or how much a bright screen has undone. A Good night you plan well can be better than an Excellent one you rush.
