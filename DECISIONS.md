# Decisions

One line per decision, with reasoning. Newest at the bottom.

- Project shell generated with XcodeGen (`project.yml`) instead of the Xcode wizard — the build runs from the command line (`xcodebuild`), which cannot drive Xcode's GUI; generated, not hand-written, so the "never hand-write a .pbxproj" intent holds.
- Source folders use Xcode synchronized folders — new files need no project-file change.
- Bundle ID prefix `com.harrypakhale`, App Group `group.com.harrypakhale.nyx` — matches the owner's GitHub handle; change in `project.yml` and both entitlements files if a different prefix is registered.
- Widget extension lives in top-level `NyxWidgets/` (not `Nyx/Widgets/`) — keeps extension sources out of the app target's synchronized folder.
- Entitlements and the widget Info.plist live in `Config/` — files there are not swept into a synchronized source folder as resources.
- App target uses Swift 6, default actor isolation MainActor, approachable concurrency — matches the current Xcode app template.
- `ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption = NO` — the app only uses HTTPS via URLSession, which is exempt.
- SwiftData container not created in the shell — Phase-owned models add it; avoids the template's throwaway `Item` model.
- Built with the installed Xcode 27.0 against an iOS 26.0 deployment target — the brief names Xcode 26; the deployment target, not the Xcode version, is what matters. iOS 27+ APIs are allowed behind availability checks with a first-class iOS 26 fallback.

## Depth and realism — 2026-10-03

Implementing every item in `Research/depth-and-realism-audit.md` before the first release, at the owner's request.

- App icon is now the "Dial" concept: the score arc and its leading star around a sphere-shaded crescent with earthshine, in three depth groups (star, arc, moon). The arc stays non-glass so its amber stays luminous; glass made it read as a hollow tube. The original moon-and-orbit icon and the Milky Way "Horizon" draft are kept in `IconSources/Concepts/`.
- The celestial gauge is now an instrument: a Liquid Glass bezel ring (`DialRing`) under the arc, a soft inner shadow so the numeral sits inside the dial, and a specular glint on the rim that slides with the phone's tilt (`MotionTilt`, gravity only, 30 Hz, only while a gauge is on screen, never under Reduce Motion or Low Power Mode). Solid dark ring under Reduce Transparency, Increase Contrast and night vision.
- The Moon is now a lit sphere drawn by a Metal shader (`Moon.metal`, `MoonView`): NASA's LRO colour map (CGI Moon Kit, 1024×512, 184 KB in the asset catalog), Lommel–Seeliger reflectance, a narrow soft terminator, and earthshine scaled by Earth's phase. It is oriented as seen from the park at the Moon's highest point that night (`moonViewTime`): bright-limb angle (Meeus 48.5) minus the parallactic angle (14.1), the lunar axis angle and optical libration (ch. 53). A test checks the drawn bright limb against the Sun's real bearing in six parks, including Alaska and American Samoa (within 3°). This replaces the earlier "never a photograph" rule for the disc: the map is real data, credited in About the data. The vector `MoonDisc` remains for the tab icon and widgets, where shaders don't run. VoiceOver says which side is lit.
- Onboarding shows the coming month's real young crescent (an hour after sunset) and the earthlit new moon over the starting park.
- The simulator runaway (load average ~770 from iOS 27 simulator daemons after hours of use) was environmental; simulators are now shut down between capture batches.
- The background sky is now real (`RealSky`, `SkyProjection`): 904 stars to magnitude 4.5 from the Yale Bright Star Catalogue (via NASA HEASARC, table security "public"; bundled as `stars.json`, 24 KB) projected stereographically for the park and night on screen, facing south (north in the southern hemisphere) at the middle of true darkness, with a ~125° vertical field. Star size and brightness follow magnitude; colour follows B−V (Ballesteros temperature) as a gentle tint over starlight. The Milky Way is a soft glow along the galactic plane (north galactic pole RA 192.859°, Dec +27.128°), brightest toward the core, drawn only when the Sun is 12° or more below the horizon; a test checks the core sits low in the south on a July night at Joshua Tree. Three depth layers (faint + Milky Way, middle, bright) drift 2/4/7 pt with the phone's tilt; the two far layers are cached Equatable canvases and only the ~20 brightest stars twinkle per frame. Screens not about one park show the starting park's sky tonight. The seeded `Starfield` remains as the fallback. Credits in About the data.
- Glass cards: `Panel` is Liquid Glass tinted deep indigo (62% indigo fill under `.regular.tint`), so the real stars glint through the edges; solid indigo under Reduce Transparency, Increase Contrast and night vision.
- Depth in motion: hero gauges on Tonight and park detail lag 8–10% behind the scroll (`DepthParallax`), floating between the fixed sky and the page; off under Reduce Motion.
- Sky arc ground is a low ridge silhouette seeded per park (illustrative, never claimed as the real skyline).
- `ImageRenderer` runs the Moon shader (verified by a test that renders a full Moon and reads a bright centre pixel), so share cards show the lit Moon beside the date over the park's real sky. Widgets can't host Metal shaders, so the app renders tonight's and tomorrow's Moon for each saved park into the App Group (`moon-<park>-<night>.png`, pruned as nights pass); the widget uses that picture and falls back to the vector Moon. The widget background draws the real stars for its park and night.
- The gauge's glass rim is now an open arc (136°–404°, round caps) like the dial itself: the full ring ran behind "DARKNESS / 100" and failed contrast on iOS 26, whose glass renders lighter. Glass cards are tinted darker (80% indigo fill) for the same reason; the iOS 26 audit passes. Earthshine raised to 9% of albedo at new moon so a young crescent's night side is faintly visible, as it is to the eye.

## Fresh audit — 2026-10-04

Four independent read-only reviews (astronomy and score math checked numerically against PyEphem; services and concurrency; UI and accessibility; configuration, data and release). Every confirmed finding is fixed below; build number is now 4.

- Moonrise/moonset use the Moon's upper limb on a refracted horizon (topocentric centre at −0.833°), not −0.3°. The old threshold put Alaska rise/set times up to 41 minutes off, missed real events and invented a Denali moonset; it also skewed the moon-below fraction in the score. New test pins sunset, astronomical dusk/dawn, moonrise and moonset to PyEphem references (Alaska, American Samoa, Grand Canyon, Joshua Tree) within 2 and 5 minutes.
- The night's sunrise is the first rise after its sunset: on short Arctic winter days the Sun can rise after local noon, which was reported as that night's sunrise.
- Milky Way guidance follows the core's highest possible altitude (90° − |latitude + 29°|): every Alaska park now says the bright center stays at or below the horizon, and no night without true darkness is told "summer favors the bright center". February counts as a pre-dawn month.
- The mean-synodic phase function (unused) is removed; phase comes from the Moon's true elongation and the comment says why it departs from the epoch method in the brief.
- Formula test pins exact points (40/25/20/15, moon bonus, renormalization, darkness cap); moon-phase tolerance tightened from 12 hours to 1.
- Forecasts are always requested for all 63 parks, never only the parks near the device: same one or two requests, and the request no longer hints at where someone is. Privacy copy (app, Info.plist purpose string, PRIVACY.md, docs/privacy.html) now says "coordinates are never sent" and discloses that park-alert requests within the Tonight radius can suggest a broad region.
- NPS requests (one shared key for every install): alerts only, except park detail which also asks for ranger programs; the /parks call is gone (descriptions are bundled); no repeat request for a park within ten minutes, even when forced; concurrent requests for one park share a single fetch. INPUT_NEEDED asks the owner to request a higher limit.
- Offline forecasts and park updates live in Application Support (excluded from backup), not Caches, which iOS empties under storage pressure; old Caches files are still read until replaced.
- Saved-park updates refresh forecasts first, then write the widget snapshot and reschedule reminders, and only then fetch park alerts, so a quick visit still updates both. The widget snapshot carries only saved parks' forecasts.
- Reminder triggers carry their Gregorian calendar, so devices set to another calendar still fire them. Turning reminders off during a reschedule no longer records the cancelled reminders as delivered.
- Siri uses whichever cached forecast is newer (widget snapshot or app cache). The widget gallery previews Joshua Tree instead of the empty state.
- A park opened from a reminder, Spotlight or widget reads night vision and Increase Contrast itself (it followed neither) and has a Done button.
- The launch starfield is removed after the reveal; it had kept animating and sensing tilt behind the tabs all session.
- The time river no longer falls back to the first night when the selected night is not on it, and a chosen night that drops off the river (detail left open past sunrise) is released, so gauge and river agree. Labels that would collide are dropped, the selected night first in priority.

- Calendar weeks start on the region's first weekday. Calendar peeks get the night-vision filter; the score tip is not shown in night vision. Accessibility-size calendar rows can open the night, as the grid can.
- Sky arc canvas ignores Smart Invert (its Moon is drawn inside it). Decorative arrows and essay symbols are hidden from VoiceOver. Journal errors clear on the next attempt. The share button keeps its place while scrubbing and renders at screen scale.

## An honest forecast — 2026-10-05

- **Third host, owner-approved (2026-10-05):** `air-quality-api.open-meteo.com` joins `developer.nps.gov` and `api.open-meteo.com`, for the CAMS aerosol forecast only. It has its own switch ("Smoke and haze", `smokeEnabled`, default on) enforced in `SafeHTTP` before any connection; `SafeHTTP.preferences` maps every host to its switch, so an unlisted host is still rejected. The product brief §13/§17 now says three hosts. Privacy label stays "Data Not Collected": like the forecast, requests carry only the 63 public park coordinates, never the device's.
- **Score unchanged.** Clouds stay the 25% term from Open-Meteo's best-match model. Model agreement, cloud layers, cold, dew, wind, visibility and smoke are context and never points; the breakdown says so. A high-cloud or smoke penalty would need validation against real nights first.
- **Model agreement:** a second forecast request (`models=gfs_seamless,ecmwf_ifs025,icon_seamless`, 7 days) gives each model's dark-window mean under the same overlap rules as clouds (full coverage or nothing, 36-hour expiry). Spread = cloudiest − clearest: ≤ 15 points agree, ≤ 35 roughly agree, otherwise disagree (15 is roughly one model's run-to-run noise; past 35 the night could go either way). All three models or none, because the copy promises three. Shown only beside a score that includes clouds, so "Clear in all three models" can never sit next to "clouds unknown"; beyond 7 days nothing is shown, never "agree".
- **Uncertainty drawn in score units:** the time river's pale bar spans the score with the clearest model's clouds to the score with the cloudiest model's (same engine), with whisker caps so it reads where the dot sits inside it; the selected night's summary adds "· 80–91" when models do not agree, and VoiceOver reads "Forecast models disagree; the score could be 80 to 91." Drawing cloud percent on a score axis would have mixed two scales.
- **Readout word:** under the Clouds meter, "Models agree" / "Roughly agree" / "Models differ" (caption, one line, scales down rather than wrapping); added to the readout's VoiceOver value.
- **Layers and the night itself:** a third forecast request (low/mid/high cloud, temperature, dew point, gusts, visibility, 7 days). Layer note only when one layer shapes the night ("Thin high cloud; bright stars only." when high ≥ 25% and low and mid < 15%). "The night itself" on park detail: coldest hour and when, "Dew likely on lenses" when air comes within 2 °C of its dew point in any dark hour, and gusts (below 15 km/h "Light wind"), via `Measurement` with `.weather`, `.wind` and `.visibility` usages so units follow the locale. Three labelled lines replaced a single " · " sentence that broke mid-phrase. Visibility appears only as a haze hint, and only when no aerosol forecast covers the night.
- **Smoke bands** (AOD 550 nm, dark-window mean): < 0.1 clear, < 0.25 light haze, < 0.5 "Haze or smoke: the Milky Way will look faint", ≥ 0.5 "Heavy smoke or haze: faint stars hidden". From 0.25 an amber caveat stands under the score on detail and Tonight, like a closure. CAMS nulls beyond ~5.5 days mean no smoke line, never "clear air". Documented in code, About the data, and attribution (CAMS via Open-Meteo, CC BY 4.0) in README and the site.
- **Caching:** `ForecastDetail` (three optional `HourlySeries`: models, layers, air) per park in `nyx-detail-<park>.json`, separate from `Forecast`, which is unchanged so old caches decode. Each series refreshes on its own six-hour clock (forced: ten minutes) and a failed request keeps the last good series. One refresh in flight at a time; later callers wait for it. Runs inside `refreshForecasts`, so every place that refreshes clouds (Tonight, Parks, detail, saved parks) refreshes detail too. The widget snapshot is unchanged: it uses only clouds.
- **Request budget:** Open-Meteo counts each coordinate as a call (more for many variables). A full refresh is clouds + models + layers + air = 4 request kinds × 63 parks ≈ 250 coordinate-calls (two chunks of ≤ 50 per kind), at most once per six hours unless pulled to refresh (then at most every ten minutes): about 1,000 a day per device in normal use, well under the free 10,000/day.
- `Forecast.mean` now calls a shared `HourlyWindow` helper (same rules, same tests) so clouds and every new series follow one definition of coverage.
- DEBUG fixtures `-nyx-state agree | disagree | smoke` (`DebugForecasts.swift`, DEBUG only) inject forecasts and detail for all parks; `-nyx-night-vision` combines night vision with any state.

## Vision Pro — 2026-10-05

"Stand under tonight's sky" (roadmap Tier 2b), on branch `vision`. The product brief's §11 "iPhone only" now reads as the iPhone app's scope; the visionOS app is a separate target.

- **Target `NyxVision`** (visionOS 26.0, bundle `com.harrypakhale.nyx.vision`, display name Nyx, own scheme). It compiles the engine by listing files (`Park`, `SkyConditions`, `AstronomyEngine`, `ScoreEngine`, `SkyAlmanac`, `WhatsUp`, the new `SkyDome`, `Moon.metal`) plus `stars.json`, `parks.json`, `sky-events.json` and the iPhone asset catalog (for `MoonMap`). Nothing from `DataServices`, `ForecastDetail` or any networking file is compiled in, so the app cannot make a request. visionOS 27 APIs are not needed; every API used was checked in the installed `XROS27.0.sdk` swiftinterfaces (all visionOS ≤ 26).
- **Score is moon and darkness only, and says so** next to the numeral and in the list header: no forecast host on visionOS v1 keeps the privacy story one sentence long ("this app makes no network requests"). The score engine is unchanged; `cloudCover: nil` renormalises as on the iPhone beyond the forecast horizon.
- **The window** is a `NavigationSplitView`: searchable park list (alphabetical, score and band beside each), and one park's night (score, the shader Moon, true darkness, the Moon's times and below-horizon share, Bortle estimate, What's up, and "The sky at 11:40 PM" written out). A bottom ornament steps nights (tonight … +365, "Back to tonight") and scrubs sunset → sunrise; "Middle of darkness" sweeps to the middle of true darkness. The written sky list is the non-immersive way to the same sky and what VoiceOver reads. A deep-indigo gradient sits over the window glass so a bright room does not wash out the night palette.
- **Immersive space** (`ImmersiveSpace`, progressive 0.35…1, opens at 1): the celestial sphere is built once in equatorial coordinates and only rotated per moment (`SkyDome.rotation`: sidereal time, latitude, facing; tested to 0.001° against `AstronomyEngine.horizontal` in four parks including American Samoa and Gates of the Arctic). Scrubbing a whole night is one transform change, so it is smooth on any hardware.
- **Stars:** the 904 Yale Bright Star Catalogue stars as one quad each in three meshes (bright < 2.2, middle < 3.6, faint), additive unlit program (`UnlitMaterial.Program` with `.add`, visionOS 2), colours from a 16-cell B−V strip drawn on device. Three meshes rather than one so twilight, moonlight and sky glow can fade the faint stars first; three draws total. No procedural filler stars: below magnitude 4.5 the sky is left honest rather than invented.
- **Milky Way:** a ribbon mesh ±20° about the galactic plane with a texture computed on device (bright, wide core and bulge, star clouds in Cygnus, Scutum and Carina, the Great Rift, mottling). A model, not a photograph, stated in code. Mesh and texture start at the anticentre so the texture seam sits where the band is faintest (it showed through the core at first). It fades with twilight, moonlight and the park's Bortle estimate.
- **The Moon** is a billboard rendered from the same `Moon.metal` shader as the window and the iPhone (`ImageRenderer`), oriented to the zenith with the real bright-limb angle at that moment, phase from the Sun's true direction. It is added as light over a disc painted in the sky's own colour at that spot, so its night side hides the stars behind it but never punches a black hole in a twilight sky (the first, opaque version did). Not a lit sphere with a `DirectionalLight`: the system's image-based light fills the night side, and the shader already does Lommel–Seeliger, libration and earthshine exactly. Drawn three times its true size so its phase reads; its place is true; the window says so. A moonlit sky brightens the dome and fades the faint stars and Milky Way.
- **Planets** are warm glows sized by magnitude with a generous collision target and a hover highlight; tapping one (or the Moon or the core) shows a name card: "Saturn · 52° up in the southeast · bright". VoiceOver reads each body's `AccessibilityComponent` ("Jupiter", "32° up in the southeast, very bright") and the dome's summary of the whole sky.
- **Orientation:** south is ahead (north in American Samoa), where the core, Moon and planets cross. The sky is not aligned to the room's real north, and the plaque at your feet says so with "Computed for <park>, <night>. Not a live view; clouds not shown." Compass letters stand on the horizon. Using the device heading would need world tracking and would put the interesting half of the sky behind most people's sofas.
- **Twilight:** the dome's colour follows the Sun's real altitude (deep blue at sunset, indigo through nautical twilight, black at −18°), brighter toward the Sun, with faint airglow and the park's sky glow at the horizon so the dark ground stands out as it does outdoors. Bright stars appear at the end of civil twilight, the faintest near nautical, the Milky Way only as astronomical twilight ends (`SkyDome.visibility`, tested monotonic).
- **Motion:** the ornament's slider is direct manipulation; "Middle of darkness" sweeps the clock on a critically damped curve matching the shared spring's feel, and under Reduce Motion it jumps. No twinkling in the immersive sky: calm, and nothing to switch off.
- **Night vision** turns window and sky red, regenerating the sky textures; the window drops glass for a near-black red panel. In the sky the textures go grey and the material tint makes them red: red baked into an additive texture rendered pale pink to white. In the window the ink is white under the red filter (as on the iPhone); a red ink filtered again read as a dim, low-contrast red.
- **Colour fidelity:** every sky material skips post-process tone mapping, which bleached bright stars and night vision's red toward white.
- **Labels** (core, name cards, compass, plaque) are SwiftUI views (`SkyLabel`) rendered into textures on quads, re-rendered only when their words change, 10 m out and scaled to read like text at a metre (compass letters 12 m on the horizon). `ViewAttachmentComponent` was tried first: far out in the sky, attachments appeared late or not at all in testing. Their meaning reaches VoiceOver through each entity's `AccessibilityComponent`.
- **Strings** live in `NyxVision/Resources/Localizable.xcstrings`, synced by `Scripts/sync_vision_catalog.py` from the visionOS build. A separate catalog because `sync_catalog.py` prunes keys the iPhone build does not extract.
- **Icon:** a layered `AppIcon.solidimagestack` (back: violet-to-black fill; middle: earthlit disc and crescent; front: score arc and leading star) rendered from the Icon Composer layers in `Nyx/Resources/AppIcon.icon/Assets`.
- **App Store:** a native visionOS build joins the iPhone record only with the *same* bundle ID (universal purchase); `.vision` would need a second record and a different name. Kept `.vision` for development as briefed; switching is one line in `project.yml` (INPUT_NEEDED).
- DEBUG launch arguments: `-nyx-vision-immersive`, `-nyx-vision-skyonly` (closes the window for sky captures), `-nyx-vision-look yaw,pitch` (turns the scene for a capture), `-nyx-vision-time 0…1`, `-nyx-vision-body saturn`, `-nyx-vision-uvtest`, plus the shared `-nyx-date`, `-nyx-park`, `-nyx-night-vision`, `-nyx-ax5`.

- 2026-10-06 · Integrator: the visionOS target uses the iPhone app's bundle ID (`com.harrypakhale.nyx`) so Vision Pro joins the existing App Store record as one universal purchase, replacing the iPhone app's compatibility mode there.

## Accessibility depth — 2026-10-06

Roadmap Tier 1 §5, "The sky for everyone", on branch `a11y`. APIs verified in the iPhoneOS 27.0 SDK before use.
- **One reader for the newer settings.** `nyxAccessibility()` (`Nyx/DesignSystem/NyxAccess.swift`) reads Differentiate Without Color, `accessibilityReduceHighlightingEffects` and `accessibilityPrefersCrossFadeTransitions` (iOS 26.4) and `systemPrefersReducedResourceUsage` (iOS 27) into one `\.nyxAccess` value at the root, on the park sheet opened from reminders and on field mode's UIKit host. The struct lives in `NyxTheme.swift` because the widget and watch share the starfield files; the reader stays app-only. Each newer setting sits in its own availability-gated modifier; iOS 26.0–26.3 read only Differentiate Without Color. DEBUG flags force each: `-nyx-differentiate`, `-nyx-reduce-highlighting`, `-nyx-crossfade`, `-nyx-reduced-resources`.
- **Audio Graphs on three charts, not four.** `AXChartDescriptor` via a lazy `AXChartDescriptorRepresentable` (built only when VoiceOver asks): the time river (score per night, estimates labelled, the clearest and cloudiest models' scores as two more series when they differ, shower or eclipse names in point labels), the calendar month (on the month's title, which is a header element; the grid itself is many elements), and the sky arc (Sun, Moon with its illumination, core, in degrees every 15 minutes, park-local times). The week strip has none: it sits inside a row that is one combined element and already speaks its best night; seven points do not need a graph.
- **The river's "best nights" are actions, not a rotor.** A rotor entry must point at its own element and the river is one adjustable element, so duplicate entries would trap the rotor. The river gets "Best night", "Next of the best nights" and "Feel the Moon" actions; the calendar (one element per night) gets real rotors, "Best nights" (tonight onward, Excellent or better, else the three best) and "Moon window". Parks gets "Closures" and "Pristine nights"; field mode gets "Milestones".
- **Sonification is rendered, not synthesized live.** `NightSonification` (`Nyx/Services/NightSound.swift`) renders twelve seconds of mono audio in pure Swift off the main thread; `AVAudioPlayerNode` plays the buffer. The brief suggested `AVAudioSourceNode`, but its render block would run Swift on the real-time thread (the SDK marks the real-time-safe variant unavailable to Swift), and a precomputed buffer is deterministic and testable. iOS 27 uses the throwing `connectNode`; iOS 26 the older `connect`. The roadmap's `installAudioTap(tapProvider:)` has no use here (nothing is recorded).
- **Mapping.** Pitch follows darkness, an octave from 330 Hz at sunset to 165 Hz once the Sun is 18° down (lower is darker, the way the sky deepens); a soft fifth and sub-octave make it a pad, with a slow breath. The Moon lowers the volume by up to 60% in proportion to illumination, eased over its first 3° of altitude. Moonrise is a pulse gliding up, moonset gliding down; the core rising (or already up at true darkness) is a small three-partial chime. Sunset to sunrise, or the cloud window under the midnight sun, which the opening line says has no true darkness. Planets and showers are left out: four sounds are learnable, ten are noise.
- **`.playback`, only on a tap.** The sound is content the person asked for, often with VoiceOver, where Silent mode muting it would make the button look broken. `.duckOthers` lowers music rather than stopping it, and deactivation notifies other apps. Nothing ever plays on its own. In field mode it lives in the options menu (headphones are kinder to others at a star party).
- **VoiceOver hears it too.** The opening line is announced; each moment is announced at low priority as it plays, only while VoiceOver runs. The transcript (one line per moment, with a key to the sounds) is always beside the button and brightens line by line as it plays, so it doubles as captions.
- **Feel the Moon.** `MoonTexture` maps illumination to Core Haptics: 3 sharp taps (sharpness 1) at new Moon to 12 soft ones (0.25) over a broad hum (intensity 0.8, sharpness 0.05) at full; a waxing Moon's hum swells and a waning one's fades. River detents go from a soft thud (0.3/0.15) at 0 to a crisp click (0.9/1) at 100. After a scrub rests 0.38 s, the night's Moon plays as a 0.6 s texture. A "Feel the Moon" button sits in the detail's Moonlight card. Core Haptics only where `supportsHaptics`; otherwise, or with the new in-app switch off (Settings › Accessibility › Sound and touch), the river keeps `sensoryFeedback(.selection)`. There is no public API to read iOS's System Haptics switch, so the in-app switch is the person's control.
- **Differentiate Without Color.** One shape vocabulary, `NightMark`: Excellent and Pristine nights become four-pointed stars on the calendar, river and week strip (hollow still means no forecast); the river's three best nights gain a small triangle beneath their glow (rings overlapped on neighbouring nights); moonlit hours on the sky arc are hatched as well as lighter, and the Sun's path is named; past calendar nights are struck through. Legends say so only when it is on. Everything else already carried text or shape (bands are words, closures and smoke have icons and text, agreement is words plus a bar).
- **Reduce Highlighting Effects** multiplies decorative glow by 0.35: the gauge's arc glow and leading-star halo, the river's peak glow, the calendar's new-moon halo, the Milky Way on the real sky and the sky arc; the gauge's specular glint and the pull-to-refresh shooting star are omitted.
- **Cross-fade** replaces the park zoom with `NavigationTransition.crossFade` on iOS 27 and the standard push on iOS 26.4, and the calendar's month slide with a fade. **Reduced resources** (iOS 27) stops twinkling, tilt and the gauge's orbiting stars, and halves the procedural starfield.
- **SSML (iOS 27)** via `AttributedString.accessibilitySpeechSSML` on a `Text` label: "Bortle" as /ˈbɔɹtəl/, "Sagittarius A*" as "A star", "Gemínidas" in Spanish for later. Applied to the detail's Bortle row; other labels already read naturally ("94 out of 100"). Fragments are unwrapped (no `<speak>`), as the header documents.
- **Voice Control.** `accessibilityInputLabels` on the river ("River", "Nights"), its AX stepper ("Night"), the gauge ("Score"), calendar nights (day number, or weekday and day), month arrows, save, filter, the sky arc, Listen, Feel the Moon and field mode's icon buttons.
- **Learn:** a sixth essay, "Stargazing for everyone" (access questions to ask a park, darkness as a barrier, red light, sound and touch in Nyx). Its own essay rather than folded into "Sharing the night", which is about etiquette; it says nothing about step-free spots in Nyx until that data is wired.
