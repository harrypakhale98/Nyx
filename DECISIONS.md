# Decisions

One line per decision, with reasoning. Newest at the bottom.

- Project shell generated with XcodeGen (`project.yml`) instead of the Xcode wizard — the build runs from the command line (`xcodebuild`), which cannot drive Xcode's GUI; generated, not hand-written, so the "never hand-write a .pbxproj" intent holds.
- Source folders use Xcode synchronized folders — new files need no project-file change.
- Bundle ID prefix `com.harrypakhale`, App Group `group.com.harrypakhale.nyx` — matches the owner's GitHub handle; change in `project.yml` and both entitlements files if a different prefix is registered.
- Widget extension lives in top-level `NyxWidgets/` (not `Nyx/Widgets/`) — keeps extension sources out of the app target's synchronized folder.
- Entitlements and the widget Info.plist live in `Config/` — files there are not swept into a synchronized source folder as resources.
- App target uses Swift 6, default actor isolation MainActor, approachable concurrency — matches the current Xcode app template.
- `ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption = NO` — the app only uses HTTPS via URLSession, which is exempt.
- SwiftData container not created in the shell — Phase-owned models add it; avoids the template's throwaway `Item` model.
- Built with the installed Xcode 27.0 against an iOS 26.0 deployment target — the brief names Xcode 26; the deployment target, not the Xcode version, is what matters. iOS 27+ APIs are allowed behind availability checks with a first-class iOS 26 fallback.

## Depth and realism — 2026-10-03

Implementing every item in `Research/depth-and-realism-audit.md` before the first release, at the owner's request.

- App icon is now the "Dial" concept: the score arc and its leading star around a sphere-shaded crescent with earthshine, in three depth groups (star, arc, moon). The arc stays non-glass so its amber stays luminous; glass made it read as a hollow tube. The original moon-and-orbit icon and the Milky Way "Horizon" draft are kept in `IconSources/Concepts/`.
- The celestial gauge is now an instrument: a Liquid Glass bezel ring (`DialRing`) under the arc, a soft inner shadow so the numeral sits inside the dial, and a specular glint on the rim that slides with the phone's tilt (`MotionTilt`, gravity only, 30 Hz, only while a gauge is on screen, never under Reduce Motion or Low Power Mode). Solid dark ring under Reduce Transparency, Increase Contrast and night vision.
- The Moon is now a lit sphere drawn by a Metal shader (`Moon.metal`, `MoonView`): NASA's LRO colour map (CGI Moon Kit, 1024×512, 184 KB in the asset catalog), Lommel–Seeliger reflectance, a narrow soft terminator, and earthshine scaled by Earth's phase. It is oriented as seen from the park at the Moon's highest point that night (`moonViewTime`): bright-limb angle (Meeus 48.5) minus the parallactic angle (14.1), the lunar axis angle and optical libration (ch. 53). A test checks the drawn bright limb against the Sun's real bearing in six parks, including Alaska and American Samoa (within 3°). This replaces the earlier "never a photograph" rule for the disc: the map is real data, credited in About the data. The vector `MoonDisc` remains for the tab icon and widgets, where shaders don't run. VoiceOver says which side is lit.
- Onboarding shows the coming month's real young crescent (an hour after sunset) and the earthlit new moon over the starting park.
- The simulator runaway (load average ~770 from iOS 27 simulator daemons after hours of use) was environmental; simulators are now shut down between capture batches.
- The background sky is now real (`RealSky`, `SkyProjection`): 904 stars to magnitude 4.5 from the Yale Bright Star Catalogue (via NASA HEASARC, table security "public"; bundled as `stars.json`, 24 KB) projected stereographically for the park and night on screen, facing south (north in the southern hemisphere) at the middle of true darkness, with a ~125° vertical field. Star size and brightness follow magnitude; colour follows B−V (Ballesteros temperature) as a gentle tint over starlight. The Milky Way is a soft glow along the galactic plane (north galactic pole RA 192.859°, Dec +27.128°), brightest toward the core, drawn only when the Sun is 12° or more below the horizon; a test checks the core sits low in the south on a July night at Joshua Tree. Three depth layers (faint + Milky Way, middle, bright) drift 2/4/7 pt with the phone's tilt; the two far layers are cached Equatable canvases and only the ~20 brightest stars twinkle per frame. Screens not about one park show the starting park's sky tonight. The seeded `Starfield` remains as the fallback. Credits in About the data.
- Glass cards: `Panel` is Liquid Glass tinted deep indigo (62% indigo fill under `.regular.tint`), so the real stars glint through the edges; solid indigo under Reduce Transparency, Increase Contrast and night vision.
- Depth in motion: hero gauges on Tonight and park detail lag 8–10% behind the scroll (`DepthParallax`), floating between the fixed sky and the page; off under Reduce Motion.
- Sky arc ground is a low ridge silhouette seeded per park (illustrative, never claimed as the real skyline).
- `ImageRenderer` runs the Moon shader (verified by a test that renders a full Moon and reads a bright centre pixel), so share cards show the lit Moon beside the date over the park's real sky. Widgets can't host Metal shaders, so the app renders tonight's and tomorrow's Moon for each saved park into the App Group (`moon-<park>-<night>.png`, pruned as nights pass); the widget uses that picture and falls back to the vector Moon. The widget background draws the real stars for its park and night.
- The gauge's glass rim is now an open arc (136°–404°, round caps) like the dial itself: the full ring ran behind "DARKNESS / 100" and failed contrast on iOS 26, whose glass renders lighter. Glass cards are tinted darker (80% indigo fill) for the same reason; the iOS 26 audit passes. Earthshine raised to 9% of albedo at new moon so a young crescent's night side is faintly visible, as it is to the eye.

## Fresh audit — 2026-10-04

Four independent read-only reviews (astronomy and score math checked numerically against PyEphem; services and concurrency; UI and accessibility; configuration, data and release). Every confirmed finding is fixed below; build number is now 4.

- Moonrise/moonset use the Moon's upper limb on a refracted horizon (topocentric centre at −0.833°), not −0.3°. The old threshold put Alaska rise/set times up to 41 minutes off, missed real events and invented a Denali moonset; it also skewed the moon-below fraction in the score. New test pins sunset, astronomical dusk/dawn, moonrise and moonset to PyEphem references (Alaska, American Samoa, Grand Canyon, Joshua Tree) within 2 and 5 minutes.
- The night's sunrise is the first rise after its sunset: on short Arctic winter days the Sun can rise after local noon, which was reported as that night's sunrise.
- Milky Way guidance follows the core's highest possible altitude (90° − |latitude + 29°|): every Alaska park now says the bright center stays at or below the horizon, and no night without true darkness is told "summer favors the bright center". February counts as a pre-dawn month.
- The mean-synodic phase function (unused) is removed; phase comes from the Moon's true elongation and the comment says why it departs from the epoch method in the brief.
- Formula test pins exact points (40/25/20/15, moon bonus, renormalization, darkness cap); moon-phase tolerance tightened from 12 hours to 1.
- Forecasts are always requested for all 63 parks, never only the parks near the device: same one or two requests, and the request no longer hints at where someone is. Privacy copy (app, Info.plist purpose string, PRIVACY.md, docs/privacy.html) now says "coordinates are never sent" and discloses that park-alert requests within the Tonight radius can suggest a broad region.
- NPS requests (one shared key for every install): alerts only, except park detail which also asks for ranger programs; the /parks call is gone (descriptions are bundled); no repeat request for a park within ten minutes, even when forced; concurrent requests for one park share a single fetch. INPUT_NEEDED asks the owner to request a higher limit.
- Offline forecasts and park updates live in Application Support (excluded from backup), not Caches, which iOS empties under storage pressure; old Caches files are still read until replaced.
- Saved-park updates refresh forecasts first, then write the widget snapshot and reschedule reminders, and only then fetch park alerts, so a quick visit still updates both. The widget snapshot carries only saved parks' forecasts.
- Reminder triggers carry their Gregorian calendar, so devices set to another calendar still fire them. Turning reminders off during a reschedule no longer records the cancelled reminders as delivered.
- Siri uses whichever cached forecast is newer (widget snapshot or app cache). The widget gallery previews Joshua Tree instead of the empty state.
- A park opened from a reminder, Spotlight or widget reads night vision and Increase Contrast itself (it followed neither) and has a Done button.
- The launch starfield is removed after the reveal; it had kept animating and sensing tilt behind the tabs all session.
- The time river no longer falls back to the first night when the selected night is not on it, and a chosen night that drops off the river (detail left open past sunrise) is released, so gauge and river agree. Labels that would collide are dropped, the selected night first in priority.

- Calendar weeks start on the region's first weekday. Calendar peeks get the night-vision filter; the score tip is not shown in night vision. Accessibility-size calendar rows can open the night, as the grid can.
- Sky arc canvas ignores Smart Invert (its Moon is drawn inside it). Decorative arrows and essay symbols are hidden from VoiceOver. Journal errors clear on the next attempt. The share button keeps its place while scrubbing and renders at screen scale.

## An honest forecast — 2026-10-05

- **Third host, owner-approved (2026-10-05):** `air-quality-api.open-meteo.com` joins `developer.nps.gov` and `api.open-meteo.com`, for the CAMS aerosol forecast only. It has its own switch ("Smoke and haze", `smokeEnabled`, default on) enforced in `SafeHTTP` before any connection; `SafeHTTP.preferences` maps every host to its switch, so an unlisted host is still rejected. The product brief §13/§17 now says three hosts. Privacy label stays "Data Not Collected": like the forecast, requests carry only the 63 public park coordinates, never the device's.
- **Score unchanged.** Clouds stay the 25% term from Open-Meteo's best-match model. Model agreement, cloud layers, cold, dew, wind, visibility and smoke are context and never points; the breakdown says so. A high-cloud or smoke penalty would need validation against real nights first.
- **Model agreement:** a second forecast request (`models=gfs_seamless,ecmwf_ifs025,icon_seamless`, 7 days) gives each model's dark-window mean under the same overlap rules as clouds (full coverage or nothing, 36-hour expiry). Spread = cloudiest − clearest: ≤ 15 points agree, ≤ 35 roughly agree, otherwise disagree (15 is roughly one model's run-to-run noise; past 35 the night could go either way). All three models or none, because the copy promises three. Shown only beside a score that includes clouds, so "Clear in all three models" can never sit next to "clouds unknown"; beyond 7 days nothing is shown, never "agree".
- **Uncertainty drawn in score units:** the time river's pale bar spans the score with the clearest model's clouds to the score with the cloudiest model's (same engine), with whisker caps so it reads where the dot sits inside it; the selected night's summary adds "· 80–91" when models do not agree, and VoiceOver reads "Forecast models disagree; the score could be 80 to 91." Drawing cloud percent on a score axis would have mixed two scales.
- **Readout word:** under the Clouds meter, "Models agree" / "Roughly agree" / "Models differ" (caption, one line, scales down rather than wrapping); added to the readout's VoiceOver value.
- **Layers and the night itself:** a third forecast request (low/mid/high cloud, temperature, dew point, gusts, visibility, 7 days). Layer note only when one layer shapes the night ("Thin high cloud; bright stars only." when high ≥ 25% and low and mid < 15%). "The night itself" on park detail: coldest hour and when, "Dew likely on lenses" when air comes within 2 °C of its dew point in any dark hour, and gusts (below 15 km/h "Light wind"), via `Measurement` with `.weather`, `.wind` and `.visibility` usages so units follow the locale. Three labelled lines replaced a single " · " sentence that broke mid-phrase. Visibility appears only as a haze hint, and only when no aerosol forecast covers the night.
- **Smoke bands** (AOD 550 nm, dark-window mean): < 0.1 clear, < 0.25 light haze, < 0.5 "Haze or smoke: the Milky Way will look faint", ≥ 0.5 "Heavy smoke or haze: faint stars hidden". From 0.25 an amber caveat stands under the score on detail and Tonight, like a closure. CAMS nulls beyond ~5.5 days mean no smoke line, never "clear air". Documented in code, About the data, and attribution (CAMS via Open-Meteo, CC BY 4.0) in README and the site.
- **Caching:** `ForecastDetail` (three optional `HourlySeries`: models, layers, air) per park in `nyx-detail-<park>.json`, separate from `Forecast`, which is unchanged so old caches decode. Each series refreshes on its own six-hour clock (forced: ten minutes) and a failed request keeps the last good series. One refresh in flight at a time; later callers wait for it. Runs inside `refreshForecasts`, so every place that refreshes clouds (Tonight, Parks, detail, saved parks) refreshes detail too. The widget snapshot is unchanged: it uses only clouds.
- **Request budget:** Open-Meteo counts each coordinate as a call (more for many variables). A full refresh is clouds + models + layers + air = 4 request kinds × 63 parks ≈ 250 coordinate-calls (two chunks of ≤ 50 per kind), at most once per six hours unless pulled to refresh (then at most every ten minutes): about 1,000 a day per device in normal use, well under the free 10,000/day.
- `Forecast.mean` now calls a shared `HourlyWindow` helper (same rules, same tests) so clouds and every new series follow one definition of coverage.
- DEBUG fixtures `-nyx-state agree | disagree | smoke` (`DebugForecasts.swift`, DEBUG only) inject forecasts and detail for all parks; `-nyx-night-vision` combines night vision with any state.

## Vision Pro polish — 2026-10-06

The immersive sky, on branch `vision-polish` (only `NyxVision/` changed). Before: `Research/Screenshots/vision-sky-*.png`; after: `vision-polish-*.png`.

- **Night is near black.** The dome's colour is now its own model (`SkyTextures.skyColor`), not `SkyDome.skyColor`: a night floor of about sRGB 5, airglow faint green-brown that peaks some degrees up (van Rhijn) and dims again in thick air, the park's Bortle glow warm and low. - **Twilight from the Sun's real altitude only:** a deep-blue zenith (own stops), a glow toward the Sun that reddens and sinks as the Sun goes down (orange at −3°, a dull ember at −12°, nothing at −18°), and, while the Sun is 0–7° down, the Earth's shadow rising opposite under a pink Belt of Venus. All of it is real twilight; none of it appears at night.
- **Moonlight is glare, not illumination:** `moonGlare = illumination^2.3`, eased in over the Moon's first degrees and dimmer low down (a quarter Moon is about a tenth of a full one). It turns the sky a dim blue (moonlight is sunlight), fades faint then middle stars, and washes the Milky Way out in proportion (a quarter Moon high hides about half, a full Moon nearly all). A 56° aureole and a phase-scaled halo surround the Moon; the aureole texture is dithered so it does not band.
- **The Milky Way is a GPU model, drawn once.** `nyxMilkyWay` (`NyxVision/Sky/SkyShaders.metal`) renders a 4096×512 galactic-coordinate texture through `ImageRenderer` in about 50 ms (the same model on the CPU took 19 s in a debug build): thin disc widening toward the core, a boxy bulge south of the plane, named star clouds (Large and Small Sagittarius, Scutum, Cygnus, Norma, Centaurus, Carina), domain-warped clumping, a fine grain of unresolved stars, and dust (the Great Rift from the Northern Coalsack to Ophiuchus, lanes across the bulge, the Pipe and ρ Ophiuchi clouds, the Coalsack, filaments along the plane). Warm toward the core, cooler in the arms, reddened at lane edges. Positions are the catalogue's galactic frame; the shapes are a model and the code says so. Seam still at the anticentre; orientation checked against the Mac preview of the same galactic transform (Cygnus–Aquila–Sagittarius, rift on the north side, Antares beside the core).
- **Stars have a hierarchy.** A 16-colour × 12-brightness atlas (`nyxStarAtlas`, parameters from `SkyTextures.look`): peak brightness rises 10^0.24 per magnitude (a perceptual curve; linear flux would crush the faint end), then saturated stars grow a glare halo, and the 15 brighter than magnitude 1 a faint wide glow. Colours from a blackbody fit of the B−V temperature (Antares orange, Rigel and Vega blue-white); faint stars desaturate, as rods see no colour. Quad size per star follows its row. Still 904 catalogue stars and three draws; no invented stars.
- **No scintillation.** It needs per-frame work on many stars (no custom shaders in RealityKit on visionOS without a Reality Composer Pro material), and the earlier decision stands: calm, nothing to switch off.
- **Skyline:** two seeded ranges (`SkyTextures.skyline`, per park, every 0.5°), the far one taking a third of the horizon's colour as haze; nearer than the stars, so the Moon and stars rise from behind it. Illustrative, and the plaque now says "the skyline is illustrative". Compass letters sit on the land just below it, dimmer (0.5; 0.95 with Increase Contrast).
- **Night vision** keeps the natural-colour textures and tints the materials red (no texture rebuild on toggle); dome and land go through the palette's red mapping. Labels no longer write depth, which had cut a dark box out of the Milky Way behind "Milky Way core".
- **Reduce Transparency** (`VisionPalette.solid`): the window's gradient and fact cards become nearly opaque, sky name cards near-opaque (also under Increase Contrast).
- **Review fix (integrator): a hard edge along the Milky Way.** Cause: the additive textures carried alpha equal to their brightness, and RealityKit un-premultiplies a texture on upload, so every faint pixel became a bright one and the glow stopped dead where its 8-bit alpha rounded to zero (a test pattern put the edge exactly on the band's ±20° mesh boundary). The Milky Way, star atlas, glow and aureole textures are now opaque (additive light ignores alpha), the band is ±30° (4096×768) fading to nothing over its outer 12°, and the bulge has a broad, faint outer glow instead of a plateau, which also removed the column look. Star and Milky Way levels were re-set for the true (dimmer) profiles.
- **Iteration without the simulator:** the textures and the sky colour were tuned with a Mac harness that compiles `SkyShaders.metal` and `SkyTextures.swift` and composites the sky for a park and moment; the simulator (load-gated) was used for the final captures only.

## Delight — 2026-10-06

Roadmap Tier 2 "delight", on branch `delight`. The score formula is unchanged; everything here is a reason to go, or a memory of having gone.

- **Trip planner as dynamic programming, not greedy.** `TripPlanner.plan` maximises the summed score over (night × park) with a straight-line hop limit between back-to-back nights (default 300 mi, user-adjustable 100–500); greedy picks a dazzling first night that strands the rest. Ties go to the shorter total distance (stay put), then park id, so the same inputs always give the same plan. ≤ 14 nights × 63² parks ≈ 56k steps, run off the main thread.
- **Weekend nights are independent trips.** With "Weekends only" (Friday and Saturday nights) the hop limit applies only between consecutive calendar nights; you go home between weekends. Up to 14 weekend nights, so the last-night picker reaches ten weeks out.
- **One measure per night.** If any candidate park lacks a cloud forecast for a night, every park that night is compared on Moon and darkness alone and labelled so; a renormalised score and a cloud-inclusive score are not comparable.
- **Closures cost 10 points in the assignment only.** A park whose last cached NPS update lists a closure is chosen only if it is clearly darker, and is always flagged with the alert; the displayed score is never altered. Nothing is fetched by the planner: it plans from what Nyx already has.
- **Start point never asks for location.** Device location is offered only if already authorised (`OneShotLocation` never prompts and does not change Tonight's starting point); otherwise the starting park.

## Backend audit — 2026-10-06

Four independent reviews (astronomy against a full 120-term Meeus reference over all 63 parks × 2026–2027; score and planners; network and data against live NPS and Open-Meteo responses; persistence, notifications and extensions). Every live response decodes; sun times agree within 0.22 min and moonrise within 6.1 min. Fixes, each with a test where it is testable:

- **Late Arctic sunrise.** Nights run noon to noon, but in western Alaska's winter solar noon is after 13:00, so Kobuk and Gates nights lost their sunrise (and with it Venus, dawn twilight in field mode and on Vision Pro). The search now continues six hours past the window.
- **Midnight sun cloud window** is the four hours around the Sun's lowest point (`SkyConditions.lowestSun`), not 22:00–02:00 on the clock (at Kobuk the Sun is 14° up at 22:00 and lowest at 02:37).
- **Hourly values are timed as Open-Meteo documents them:** clouds, temperature, dew point, visibility and aerosols are instant values (each stands for the half hour around its timestamp); gusts are the preceding hour's maximum. Before, every value stood for the hour after it, shifting each night's cloud average half an hour late. Scores move slightly; the formula is unchanged.
- **Refracted horizons everywhere:** eclipse visibility uses the moonrise horizon (−0.833°), planet rise and set −0.567°.
- **A picked start day is that night.** "Find the best night" (and its snippet) and Ask Nyx's best-nights tool treated a date picked at midnight, or before a western park's sunrise, as the night before; a picked day is now that day's evening (`NightPlanner.nights(_:day:)`), never before tonight.
- **Ask Nyx is told today's date in the person's calendar** (an ISO style alone printed GMT's, tomorrow in a US evening); its nearby-parks tool says when a score is moon and darkness only; an empty park name no longer matches the first park.
- **Calendar opens on tonight's month** (at 1 AM on the 1st, tonight is still last month's last night). **Tonight's ranking** breaks ties as every other surface does (a forecast before an estimate).
- **Ranger programs:** opening a park's detail right after Tonight fetched its alerts now asks for programs (events only) instead of showing "could not be checked"; events page in fifties (NPS caps `pageSize` at 50, so parks with 51+ events lost the rest); the night-sky filter also matches "astronom…", telescope, Milky Way, dark sky, constellation and meteor.
- **Back off when a server refuses** (`HTTPStatusError`, `Backoff`): an hour after 429, fifteen minutes after other errors, per host and kind, forced or not. Every install shares one NPS key (1,000 requests an hour); without this, a spent quota was retried on every screen visit by every install. Being offline or a switch turned off never waits.
- **Saved-park changes take effect at once.** `updateSaved` publishes the widget snapshot, watch context and reminders from cached forecasts first, then again only if forecasts changed; a change made during a run (unsave, shower reminders off) is queued, not dropped.
- **Reminders:** ones that no longer qualify are cancelled even while notifications are off in Settings (iOS keeps pending ones); triggers carry seconds, so "in a minute" cannot round into the past.
- **Live Activity times are in the park's zone** (`FieldActivityAttributes.timeZoneID`, optional so running activities still decode), matching field mode.

- **Journal:** a new entry starts on the latest night that has begun (`Park.lastNightBegun`), so one written at 1 AM or over breakfast records last night and its Moon.

## Ranking beyond the forecast: usual clouds — 2026-10-06

- **The problem.** Every list of nights (Find the best night and its snippet, the large widget's ringed night, Smart Stack, Ask Nyx, Tonight, the time river's glow, the calendar rotor, the trip planner) compared a night with a cloud forecast against a night beyond it by score. Beyond the forecast the score drops clouds and renormalises (§8), which on a dark night is within a point of a perfectly clear sky, so far nights won against near nights with ordinary forecast cloud (a 92 estimate over a 90 new-moon night at 20% cloud), and Olympic in November tied Death Valley.
- **Options weighed with the owner:** keep it; forecast nights first (hides the coming new moon); Moon and darkness for all (ignores a known overcast); a two-part answer (honest, leaves the judgement to the person); calibrated odds of a great night (most machinery, least certain numbers). **Chosen:** rank by expected darkness using each park's usual clouds.
- **Data.** `Nyx/Resources/cloud-climate.json` (16 KB), built offline by `Scripts/build_cloud_climate.py` from ERA5 hourly total cloud cover 2015–2024 (Copernicus Climate Change Service, CC BY 4.0; point time series at each park's own coordinates, the ones the forecast uses), averaged over each night's cloud window by the app's own rule. Per park and month: mean cloud and the share of nights under 30% (mostly clear). Downloaded with the owner's Climate Data Store account; the key stays in `~/.cdsapirc` and is never read or written by the script. Checked against known climates (Olympic November 79% cloud, Death Valley September 87% of nights mostly clear, the Everglades' wet season, American Samoa's December–March). No runtime request, no new host.
- **Rule.** `Night.rankScore`: the score with a cloud forecast; beyond it, the score this night would have under the park's usual cloud for that month (an expected value). `NightPlanner.better`/`ranked` and every ranking above use it; on a tie, the forecast night first. The **displayed score never changes** and stays "Moon and darkness only" where it is (§8 unchanged for display); the departure from §8 is in ranking only.
- **Words.** Beside every "Moon and darkness only" caption (detail, calendar peek and list, river, Find the best night dialog and snippet, trip reasons, Ask Nyx records): "About 8 in 10 November nights here are mostly clear." (tenths; "rarely" at 0, "nearly always" at 10). About the data gains "Usual clouds" with the method and the required Copernicus attribution; its Forecasts block now describes the midnight-sun window correctly (the four hours around the Sun's lowest point). Trip planner note: parks are compared with their usual clouds for that month.
- **Scope.** iPhone app and widgets (the widget target bundles the table). Watch and Vision Pro unchanged: the watch compares tonight only, and Vision Pro's list is explicitly moon and darkness only.

## Award audit implementation, wave 1 — 2026-10-07

### Integration
- **The score's hard caps stay hard.** Every clear moonless night at a Bortle 3 park reads 89, because the cap is the honest ceiling for that sky; night-to-night order within a park comes from the tie-breaks (measured glow, then minutes of true darkness, then model spread). A softer cap would let a Bortle 3 sky drift toward "Pristine", which is what the audit found overstated.
- **Expected scores beyond the forecast use the cloud cap on the month's mean cloud.** Because the cap is concave, the true expectation is at most this, so the number never overstates the night.
- **T Coronae Borealis is out of `stars.json`.** The Bright Star Catalogue lists HR 5958 at its 1946 outburst magnitude (2.0); it normally sits near magnitude 10, far below the 4.5 cut, so every sky drew a bright star beside Alphecca that is not there. 903 stars. Nothing indexes the catalogue by row.
- **Widget snapshot carries both** the score lane's forecast detail (smoke, layers) and the data lane's closures, each decoded leniently.
- **Shower reminders use the same honest range as What's up** ("60–130 an hour").

### Score and engine
- **Score v2: weakest-link caps.** The four additive parts and the brief's weights (40 moon / 25 clouds / 20 sky glow / 15 length) stay, so the breakdown keeps four reasons; the score is now min(sum, darkness cap, cloud cap, sky-glow cap, smoke cap). Departure from §5 (pure additive): additive clouds could never pull a moonless desert night below Good (audit S1). Pinned by tests.
- **Cloud cap** = 100 − 0.9 × cloud% (overcast 10, 80% 28, 50% 55, 30% 73, 10% 91). Tests: 100% cloud never ≥ 40; 80% never ≥ 60 (S1).
- **Sky-glow cap**: Bortle 1–2 → 100, 3 → 89, 4 → 84, 5 → 74, 6 → 59, 7+ → 39, so "Pristine" needs Bortle 2 or darker (S2, PC-5).
- **Smoke cap**: aerosol optical depth ≥ 0.5 → 59 (Fair at most), 0.25–0.5 → 74 (Good at most); unknown AOD caps nothing. The AOD is used whenever the air forecast covers the night, whatever its age, because it can only lower a score (S4, AS-6).
- **The breakdown names the binding cap** ("Clouds limit tonight to 55."), and the score readout under every gauge says it too, so four meters adding to 72 beside a 10 never look contradictory. Darkness keeps its existing duration sentence in the breakdown.
- **Clouds by lead time, one rule everywhere** (S3/E4, AS-4, AS-8): cloud = w·forecast + (1−w)·usual, usual = the park's ERA5 cloud for the month, w = clamp((10 − lead days)/7, 0, 1), lead = middle of the night's cloud window − forecast issue time. No forecast for the night → the usual clouds. The 36-hour forecast expiry is gone (a stale forecast fades instead of vanishing, so going offline can never raise a score).
- **Departure from brief §8**: beyond the forecast horizon the cloud term is no longer dropped and the rest renormalised ÷0.75; the displayed number is the expected score with the park's usual clouds, still marked "No cloud forecast yet" (hollow).
