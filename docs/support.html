Help under a dark sky.

Scores are planning estimates. Always confirm park access before you travel; a high score never means a road is open.

Contact. Email harry.pakhale98@gmail.com or open an issue on GitHub. Include your device (iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch or Apple Vision Pro), its software version, the park and screen, and what happened. Please don't send private journal entries or photos.

Common questions

  • "Moon and darkness only" or hollow nights. No cloud forecast is available yet. Forecasts reach about 16 days ahead; beyond that, Nyx scores moon and darkness alone and says so. Pull down to refresh when you have a connection, and check that Cloud forecasts is on under Settings → Your privacy.
  • "Access not checked." Nyx could not get current park alerts. Contact the park before traveling.
  • No nearby parks. Widen the radius or choose a different starting park. The radius is a straight line, not driving distance.
  • Location is off. Tap the starting-park name and choose a park. Location is optional.
  • No reminders. Save a park and turn on reminders in Settings. Only nights scoring 90 or more with a complete, recent cloud forecast qualify. Reminders are recalculated whenever Nyx opens.
  • "No true darkness tonight." In Alaska's summer the Sun never sinks 18° below the horizon, so astronomical darkness never arrives. Nyx caps those nights below 40.
  • Night vision. Turn it on in Settings or from Control Center. Lower your screen brightness too; Nyx does not change it for you.
  • Your journal. Entries and photos stay on your iPhone or iPad. Back up your device with Apple's settings; Nyx has no cloud sync or account recovery.
  • Search at the largest text sizes. With the largest accessibility text sizes, Parks shows its search field in the page, under its title, instead of in the navigation bar.

Apple Watch

  • "Clouds unknown" on the watch. The watch makes no network requests. Its cloud forecasts come from Nyx on your paired iPhone: open Nyx on the iPhone with a connection, and the watch updates. Without them, the watch scores moon and darkness only and says so.
  • Saved parks. The watch shows the parks you saved on your iPhone.

Apple Vision Pro

  • Opening the sky. Choose a park in the window, then "Stand under this sky". "Leave the sky" closes it. The clock at the bottom of the window steps nights and moves through the hours, and the sky follows.
  • "Moon and darkness only." Nyx on Vision Pro makes no network requests, so its score leaves out clouds. For cloud forecasts, park alerts and field mode, use Nyx on iPhone or iPad.
  • Which way is south? The sky is computed for the park and night you choose, with south ahead of you, where the Milky Way, Moon and planets cross. It is not turned to your room's real directions, and clouds are not shown. A plaque at your feet says so.
  • Names in the sky. Tap a planet, the Moon or the Milky Way core to see its name, its height and its direction.

About the data

Sun and Moon positions are calculated on your device (moonrise and moonset to about ±15 minutes). Light-pollution classes are conservative estimates, not measurements. Smoke and haze come from the Copernicus (CAMS) forecast and appear beside the score as a caveat, never as points; telescope seeing is not forecast. The Milky Way core, planets (to about a degree) and meteor showers are also calculated on your device; shower rates are a rough guide from the International Meteor Organization calendar, and lunar eclipse times come from NASA (Fred Espenak). None of them change the score. Weather data: Open-Meteo, CC BY 4.0. Air-quality data: Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service (CAMS) via Open-Meteo, CC BY 4.0. Park data: National Park Service; Nyx is not affiliated with or endorsed by it.
