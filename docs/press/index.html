Press kit

Plan a darker night.

Nyx is a free app that tells you where and when the sky will be darkest: at the 63 US national parks, at national parks and dark-sky places around the world, and at any spot you save.

The story

National parks recorded more than 323 million recreation visits in 20251, and night is part of why people come: in a survey at Acadia, nearly 90 percent of visitors agreed that viewing the night sky was important to them2. Yet a trip to see the Milky Way still fails for reasons no single app joined together: a bright Moon, a cloud deck, the glow of a distant city, or a closed road. Nyx asks one question, where should I go and on which night, and answers it with a darkness score for each park and night, explained in four plain parts. Nyx takes the same question around the world, with the US national parks still at its heart. It runs its astronomy on the device, says what it does not know, and collects nothing.

What it does

  • One darkness score per place per night, from 0 to 100: moonlight (40), clouds (25), sky glow (20) and true darkness (15), with the weakest one allowed to cap the rest, so an overcast night can never read as good.
  • Around the world. National parks and certified dark-sky places in 172 countries, with sky brightness from NASA's satellite night lights and usual clouds from ten years of weather records, both labeled as estimates. A place abroad says plainly that Nyx cannot check its closures, and links its official site. Forecasts for places abroad are asked for by whole regions, and a saved spot is sent only rounded to about 11 km (7 miles).
  • Where and when. Tonight's darkest places within a straight-line radius of where you are, a town or city, a park or a spot you saved; Plan, with a month for one park and its best stretch of nights, a thirty-night time river whose Moon changes shape as you scrub, and a planner that suggests a park for each of your free nights; and a map of all 63 US parks by tonight's score, with a world map beside it.
  • The park, in three chapters. Tonight, The sky and The place: the score and its best clear window, a full-screen view of tonight's sky with the brightest stars named, viewing spots, sky glow and where to stay, with campground links only to Recreation.gov or nps.gov.
  • Closures beside the score. At the US national parks, National Park Service alerts sit next to the number, because a dark sky is no use behind a closed road.
  • An honest forecast. At the US national parks, three weather models compared, with their range drawn on the dial when they disagree; cloud layers, dew, cold and wind through true darkness; wildfire smoke that can cap the score. Beyond about three days the forecast is eased toward each place's usual clouds for the month, and those nights say so.
  • What's up tonight. The Milky Way core timed for each park, planets, meteor showers with a rate range, and lunar eclipses the park can see.
  • Field mode. At the park, a red, dimmed screen counts down to true darkness, tracks how long your eyes have adapted, and turns the real sky to wherever you point the phone, with no camera. A Live Activity, alarms and a Control Center control come with it.
  • Apple Watch. Tonight's score with the Digital Crown to look ahead, the forecast models' range when they differ, a thirty-minute dark-adaptation clock that taps your wrist, and complications that turn red at dusk.
  • Apple Vision Pro. Stand under a US national park's computed sky on a chosen night, with star names and constellation figures, turn the night by hand, and place the Moon, with NASA's color map and relief, lit at its true phase, on your table.
  • A private journal with notes and selected photos; each night becomes a star in your own constellation. Add a night by voice with Siri. It can be exported as a file you keep.
  • Around the system. Tonight and Moon widgets, Control Center controls for night vision and field mode, a Live Activity that follows a night and its forecast, Siri and Shortcuts, Spotlight, and Handoff between your devices.

Accessibility

  • VoiceOver: every custom control, including the dial, the Moon, the time river, the sky arc and the calendar nights, speaks a summary of its data. Audio graphs let you hear a month of darkness.
  • Sound and touch: "Listen to tonight" plays the shape of the night as tones with a transcript; haptics can follow the Moon's phase. "Feel tonight" plays the night as touch; touch and hold the shape of the night to read any hour, and on iPhone feel it hum under your finger. In field mode a tone placed around you leads you toward the core.
  • Text and display: VoiceOver, Dynamic Type up to the largest accessibility sizes, Reduce Motion, Reduce Transparency, Increase Contrast, Bold Text and Smart Invert are supported. Assistive Access shows one answer in large type.
  • Night-adapted eyes: night vision for the whole app, with measured contrast (red text on black 6.2:1).
  • Getting there: step-free notes for viewing spots, each quoting the park's own accessibility page.
  • Evidence, stated plainly: Apple's automated accessibility audit passes on 32 screens of the iPhone app in both palettes in the simulator, and on ten key screens at the largest text size; a few findings it cannot sample were checked by eye. The Watch and Vision Pro apps are not covered by it. Testing on physical devices, and with people who use assistive technologies, is the next step and not yet complete.

The numbers, and where they come from

  • 63 US national parks, 85 named viewing spots, 18 International Dark Sky Parks among them (as of July 2026).
  • 3,359 national parks and certified dark-sky places in 172 countries from Wikidata (141 of them dark-sky designated), and more than 31,500 towns and cities to start from.
  • 0 data collected: no server, no analytics, no account. Four public services supply data, each with its own switch.
  • Moon rise and set times agree with the U.S. Naval Observatory to within 3.7 minutes at mid-latitude parks and to within a minute at the parks checked in Alaska, Hawaiʻi, American Samoa and the Virgin Islands; sunrise, sunset and twilight to within a minute.
  • 903 stars from the Yale Bright Star Catalogue behind every screen.
