Press kit

Plan a darker night.

Nyx is a free app that tells you where and when the sky will be darkest across the 63 US national parks.

The story

National parks recorded more than 323 million recreation visits in 20251, and night is part of why people come: in a survey at Acadia, nearly 90 percent of visitors agreed that viewing the night sky was important to them2. Yet a trip to see the Milky Way still fails for reasons no single app joined together: a bright Moon, a cloud deck, the glow of a distant city, or a closed road. Nyx asks one question, where should I go and on which night, and answers it with a Darkness Score for each park and night, explained in four plain parts. It runs its astronomy on the device, says what it does not know, and collects nothing.

What it does

  • One Darkness Score per park per night, from 0 to 100: the Moon (40), clouds (25), sky glow (20) and the hours of true darkness (15), with the weakest one allowed to cap the rest, so an overcast night can never read as good.
  • Where and when. Tonight's darkest parks within a straight-line radius; a month calendar with the best stretch of nights; a thirty-night time river whose Moon changes shape as you scrub; and a planner that suggests a park for each of your free nights.
  • Closures beside the score. National Park Service alerts sit next to the number, because a dark sky is no use behind a closed road.
  • An honest forecast. Three weather models compared, with the range drawn when they disagree; cloud layers, dew, cold and wind for the dark hours; wildfire smoke that can cap the score. Beyond about three days the forecast is eased toward each park's usual clouds for the month, and those nights say so.
  • What's up tonight. The Milky Way core timed for each park, planets, meteor showers with a rate range, and lunar eclipses the park can see.
  • Field mode. At the park, a red, dimmed screen counts down to true darkness, tracks how long your eyes have adapted, and turns the real sky to wherever you point the phone, with no camera. A Live Activity, alarms and a Control Center control come with it.
  • Apple Watch. Tonight's score with the Digital Crown to look ahead, a thirty-minute dark-adaptation clock that taps your wrist, and complications that turn red at dusk.
  • Apple Vision Pro. Stand under a park's computed sky on a chosen night, with star names and constellation figures, turn the night by hand, and place the Moon, lit at its true phase, on your table.
  • A private journal with notes and selected photos; each night becomes a star in your own constellation. It can be exported as a file you keep.

Accessibility

  • VoiceOver: every custom control, including the dial, the Moon, the time river, the sky arc and the calendar nights, speaks a summary of its data. Audio graphs let you hear a month of darkness.
  • Sound and touch: "Listen to tonight" plays the shape of the night as tones with a transcript; haptics can follow the Moon's phase.
  • Text and display: designed for the largest accessibility text sizes, Reduce Motion, Reduce Transparency, Increase Contrast and Differentiate Without Color.
  • Night-adapted eyes: a red mode for the whole app, with measured contrast (red text on black 6.2:1).
  • Getting there: step-free notes for viewing spots, each quoting the park's own accessibility page.
  • Evidence, stated plainly: Apple's automated accessibility audit passes on every screen in both palettes in the simulator. Testing on physical devices, and with people who use assistive technologies, is the next step and not yet complete.

The numbers, and where they come from

  • 63 national parks, 85 named viewing spots, 18 International Dark Sky Parks (as of July 2026).
  • 0 data collected: no server, no analytics, no account. Three public services supply data, each with its own switch.
  • Moon rise and set times agree with the U.S. Naval Observatory to within 3.7 minutes at mid-latitude parks and to within a minute at the parks checked in Alaska, Hawaiʻi, American Samoa and the Virgin Islands; sunrise, sunset and twilight to within a minute.
  • 903 stars from the Yale Bright Star Catalogue behind every screen.
