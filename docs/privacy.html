Nyx · Privacy policy

Your night stays yours.

Nyx has no account, no ads, no tracking. Your journal never leaves your phone.

Updated October 5, 2026. This policy covers the Nyx app for iPhone and Apple Watch.

What the developer receives

Nothing. Nyx has no server, account system, analytics, crash-reporting SDK or advertising. The developer never receives your journal, photos, saved parks, location or usage.

What stays on your iPhone

  • Journal and photos. Entries and the photos you choose are stored in the app on your iPhone. Nyx does not sync them to iCloud. Your device backup settings may include app data. Deleting an entry removes it; deleting the app removes its data, subject to Apple's backups.
  • Location. Only if you choose "Near me", and only while you use the app. Nyx compares straight-line distances to parks on your iPhone. Your coordinates are never sent anywhere. Cloud forecasts are requested for all 63 parks at once; park alerts are requested for parks within your radius, which can suggest a broad region, and can be switched off in Your privacy. You can always choose a starting park instead.
  • Photos. Chosen through Apple's photo picker. Nyx can see only the photos you select.
  • Reminders. Local notifications scheduled on your iPhone. Turn them off in Nyx or in iPhone Settings.
  • Apple Watch. The watch app makes no network requests. Your iPhone passes it your saved parks, the night-vision setting and the cached cloud forecasts directly, through Apple's WatchConnectivity between your paired devices. The watch works out the sky itself and keeps its settings on the watch.
  • On-device AI. On iPhones that support Apple's on-device model, optional explanations use only the records shown on screen. Nothing is sent to a remote AI service.

The three network services

Nyx contacts exactly three services, and only to update public information:

  • Open-Meteo (api.open-meteo.com) for cloud forecasts, three forecast models for comparison, cloud layers, temperature, dew point, wind and visibility. Requests contain the public coordinates of all 63 national parks, never yours.
  • Open-Meteo air quality (air-quality-api.open-meteo.com) for the CAMS aerosol forecast that warns of smoke and haze. Requests contain the same public park coordinates, never yours.
  • National Park Service (developer.nps.gov) for park alerts and ranger programs. Requests contain a park code.

Like any website, these services see your IP address and ordinary request information. Open-Meteo states that its logs are not linked to identities and are deleted after 90 days. Their own policies govern their servers: Open-Meteo, National Park Service. You can switch each service off in Nyx under Settings → Your privacy; information already saved stays available, and moon, twilight, calendar and journal keep working offline.

Children

Nyx collects no personal information from anyone, including children.
