The darkest nights, wherever you are

Where, and which night, for the darkest sky.

Nyx weighs the moonlight, the clouds, the sky glow and the hours of true darkness for each place and night, and turns them into one score from 0 to 100: for the 63 US national parks, about 3,400 national parks and certified dark-sky places in 172 countries, and any spot you save. It shows its reasons, puts US park closures beside the number, and says what it cannot know.

The darkness score

One number, with its reasons.

Four things decide whether you will see the Milky Way. Nyx adds them up for each place and night, then lets the weakest one hold the total down, because one problem is enough to spoil a night.

97 Pristine DARKNESS / 100
  • Moonlight40 / 40

    How much moonlight reaches the sky through the hours of true darkness, by the Moon's phase and height.

  • Clouds25 / 25

    Averaged over the hours of true darkness only. At the US national parks, three weather models are compared for the coming week.

  • Sky glow17 / 20

    The park's estimated Bortle class, with satellite night lights to show where the glow comes from.

  • True darkness15 / 15

    Hours with the Sun 18° below the horizon. In an Alaskan summer there are none, and Nyx says so.

The weakest part can cap the rest. Heavy cloud, a bright sky, wildfire smoke or a short night holds the score down, and Nyx names the cap under the number: "Clouds limit tonight to 55." An overcast night never reads as good.

Tonight

The darkest sky within reach.

Start from where you are, from any of about 31,500 towns and cities, from a park, or from a spot you saved. Nyx shows the best-scoring places around it tonight, with the reasons on the same screen.

  • A radius you set, measured honestly: "within 200 mi as the crow flies".
  • At the US national parks, National Park Service alerts sit beside the score; a dark sky is no use behind a closed road. Elsewhere Nyx says plainly that it cannot check closures, and links the park's own site.
  • From your starting point, Nyx also works out tonight's Moon, darkness and planets, without asking any service.

The park, in three chapters

Tonight. The sky. The place.

Each park's page reads in three chapters: tonight's score and its best window, the sky and what is in it, and the place itself, with viewing spots, sky glow and where to stay.

  • Tonight's sky, full screen: drag to turn, slide from sunset to sunrise, and see where the Milky Way stands before you drive, with the brightest stars named.
  • "Before you go" gathers the park's alerts. Campgrounds link only to Recreation.gov or nps.gov.

What's up tonight

The Milky Way, and when to look.

The galactic core timed for the park you chose, the planets with where and when they rise, meteor showers with an honest rate range, zodiacal light, satellites, and lunar eclipses the park can actually see.

  • Computed on your iPhone. Planets are placed to about a degree through 2032.
  • Times are park-local, wherever you plan from.

Plan

Choose the night worth the drive.

A month for one park, each night a small sky sized by its score, with the best five nights in a row ringed. Or tell Nyx which nights you are free, and it suggests a park for each.

A forecast is reliable for a few days. After that Nyx leans on each park's usual clouds, so every night shows which it is:

Every park tonight

The US parks, and the world.

The Parks tab draws all 63 US national parks by tonight's score, from Acadia to American Samoa, with closures marked. Around the world, places are grouped by country, with a world map. Tap a place to open it, or switch to the list.

  • Drawn on the device over a plain outline of the country, or of the world from Natural Earth. No map service learns where you look.
  • Filled, half and hollow marks tell forecast nights from usual-cloud nights here too.
  • Start from a town abroad, as in the first screen from Ishigaki in Japan, or save a spot of your own, as in the second.

Field mode

At the park, the screen turns red.

Your eyes need twenty to thirty minutes to adapt to the dark, and one glance at a bright screen undoes much of it. Field mode dims everything to deep red, counts down to true darkness and turns the real sky to wherever you point the phone, with no camera.

  • A dark-adaptation clock, alarms for true darkness and the core, and a Live Activity that follows the night from your Lock Screen.
  • Where to look by sound: a tone placed around you leads you toward the core, and follows your head with headphones that track movement.
  • Red text on black measures 6.2 : 1, and a brighter red reaches 6.9 : 1.

Journal

Every night becomes a star.

A private journal turns each night out into a star in your own constellation, and gathers your year under the stars. Add a night by voice with Siri, describe your photos, and export the journal as a file you keep.

  • Notes and photos stay on your device. Nyx has no account and no cloud sync.

For more people

Hear the night. Feel the Moon.

Every custom control in Nyx speaks a summary of its data. "Listen to tonight" plays the shape of the night as tones, with a transcript, and "Feel tonight" plays it as touch. Audio graphs let VoiceOver users hear a month of darkness.

  • Designed for VoiceOver and the largest text sizes, with night vision for night-adapted eyes and a one-answer layout for Assistive Access.
  • Step-free notes for viewing spots, quoted from the park's own accessibility page where it says something, and marked unknown where it does not.
  • City glow named: satellite night lights show which towns brighten each park's horizon, and how much of the light lies in each direction.

Nyx 2.0 brings together everything since 1.1, and now looks for the darkest nights around the world. The 63 US national parks stay at its heart, checked by hand as before.

Around the world

About 3,400 national parks and certified dark-sky places in 172 countries, each scored night by night. Sky brightness and usual clouds are labeled as estimates, and a place abroad says plainly that Nyx cannot check its closures.

Start anywhere

Any of about 31,500 towns and cities as your starting point, or a spot of your own: where you stand, a pair of coordinates or a town. Spots are scored and followed like parks.

Private by region

Forecasts for places abroad are asked for by whole regions, about the size of a country. A spot you save is sent only rounded to about 11 km (7 miles), behind its own switch.

A closer look at the sky

The Moon in relief from NASA's elevation data, tonight's sky with the brightest stars named, and the forecast models' range on the dial when they disagree.

In five languages

English, Spanish, German, French and Japanese. The translations are drafts; native speakers will review each one, and their corrections follow in updates.

Honest by design

It says what it cannot know.

A planning app is only useful if you can trust it, so Nyx draws its uncertainty instead of hiding it.

Models side by side

At the US national parks, three weather models are compared for the next week. When they disagree, Nyx draws the range and says so.

Usual clouds, labelled

Beyond about three days, the forecast eases toward each park's usual clouds for the month, from ten years of climate records, and those nights are marked.

Estimates called estimates

Bortle classes, sky glow and meteor rates carry the word. Moonrise agrees with the U.S. Naval Observatory to within minutes.

Access first

A high score never means a road is open. At the US national parks, closures sit beside the number, and if alerts can't load, Nyx says so and links to the park's current conditions. Elsewhere it says it cannot check closures.

  • air-quality-api.open-meteo.comSmoke and haze forecasts for the 63 US parks
