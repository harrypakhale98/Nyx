## Description (≤4000)

```
Stand under a darker sky.

Nyx on Apple Vision Pro shows the night sky over any of the 63 US national parks, on any night in the coming year, computed on device from the positions of the Sun, the Moon, the planets and the stars.

PLAN THE NIGHT
Browse the parks with each night's Darkness Score, from 0 to 100. Open a park to see its night: the Moon's phase and its times, the hours of true darkness, the park's estimated sky glow, and what is up, from the Milky Way core to the planets. Step through the nights ahead, scrub from sunset to sunrise, or jump to the middle of true darkness. Times are park-local.

STAND UNDER THIS SKY
Open the sky and the night surrounds you. The 904 brightest stars are placed from the Yale Bright Star Catalogue, in their true colors. The Milky Way is a model drawn from the shape of our galaxy, with its star clouds and dark lanes. The Moon shows its real phase and tilt for that park and hour. Planets glow, sized by their brightness; tap one, the Moon or the Milky Way core to see its name, its height and its direction.

THE NIGHT AS IT FALLS
Scrub the hours and the sky follows: twilight deepens through blue to black, the faintest stars appear only as twilight ends, and a bright Moon washes out the Milky Way. Each park's estimated sky glow brightens its horizon.

HONEST ABOUT WHAT IT SHOWS
The sky is computed, not a live view. South is ahead of you, where the Milky Way, Moon and planets cross, rather than your room's real south. Clouds are not shown. The skyline is illustrative. The Darkness Score here leaves out clouds, because Nyx on Vision Pro does not download forecasts: it weighs moonlight, estimated sky glow and the length of darkness. For cloud forecasts, park alerts and field mode, use Nyx on iPhone.

COMFORTABLE AT NIGHT
Night vision turns the window and the sky red. The sky moves only when you move the clock: no twinkling and no autoplay. VoiceOver describes the whole sky and names the Moon, each planet and the core, and the window lists the same sky in words. Reduce Transparency and Increase Contrast are supported.

PRIVATE BY DESIGN
No account, no advertising, no tracking, and no network requests. Everything Nyx shows on Apple Vision Pro is computed on the device.

PLEASE NOTE
Scores are estimates and do not confirm clear skies or safe access. Check current road and park conditions before traveling. Moonrise and moonset are approximate, to about 15 minutes.

Stars: Yale Bright Star Catalogue (Hoffleit and Warren, via NASA HEASARC). Moon imagery: NASA. Park data: National Park Service. Nyx is not affiliated with or endorsed by the National Park Service or NASA.
```

## Review notes (visionOS)

```
Nyx for Apple Vision Pro makes no network requests and has no login. Choose a park in the window, then "Stand under this sky" to open the immersive sky; "Leave the sky" closes it. The clock ornament at the bottom of the window steps nights and scrubs the hours, and the sky follows. Tap a planet, the Moon or the Milky Way core for its name card. The sky is computed for the chosen park and night and is not aligned to the room's real north (a plaque at your feet says so). The score leaves out clouds on Vision Pro (labeled "moon and darkness only" beside it).
```
